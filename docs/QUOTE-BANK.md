# THE QUOTE BANK

Verbatim Aperture copy, the lines fandom most often gets wrong, and a
style guide for writing new copy in the same voice.

Compiled from Portal 2 caption files (Announcer, Cave_Johnson, GLaDOS), a
Portal 1 script transcript, and the decompiled ActionScript of the 2007
aperturescience.com ARG terminal. Anything marked **uncertain** could not be
verified against a primary source and must not be set in type as canon.

> **Read the CORRECTIONS section before you quote anything.** Several of the
> most famous Aperture lines are misattributed, misquoted, or are not lines
> at all.

---

## 1. CORRECTIONS — read this first

- 'We do what we must because we can' IS NOT PAINTED ON A WALL and is not, in-game, a company motto. It is a lyric from 'Still Alive', the Portal 1 end-credits song. Sung line breaks: 'Aperture Science / We do what we must / Because we can / For the good of all of us / Except the ones who are dead'. Fandom and later merchandise turned it into a motto. You may absolutely use it as 1959 wall text — just know you are inventing that placement.

- 'For the good of all of us, except FOR the ones who are dead' — the 'for' is wrong. The lyric is 'Except the ones who are dead'.

- 'THE CAKE IS A LIE' IS NEVER SPOKEN BY ANYONE. GLaDOS never says it. It is graffiti scrawled repeatedly on the walls of the hidden 'rat man' dens in Portal 1, written by a hiding employee. Quoting it as a GLaDOS line, or as Aperture corporate copy, is the single most common Portal error.

- The apocalypse / emergency-testing lines are the ENRICHMENT CENTER ANNOUNCER, not GLaDOS. 'Testing is the future, and the future starts with you', the Laws of Robotics gag, the smooth jazz, the animal-king line, 'future-starter' — all announcer, in Portal 2's pre-hub, on pre-recorded tape while GLaDOS is offline. Attribute them correctly.

- 'The Enrichment Center reminds you that the Weighted Companion Cube will never threaten to stab you and, in fact, cannot speak.' — commonly misquoted as 'cannot talk' or with the clauses reordered. Also note the follow-up is a SEPARATE line: 'In the event that the Weighted Companion Cube does speak, the Enrichment Center urges you to disregard its advice.' They are frequently merged into one quote.

- 'Science isn't about WHY. It's about WHY NOT.' is only the opening. The full line continues: 'Why is so much of our science dangerous? Why not marry safe science if you love it so much. In fact, why not invent a special safety door that won't hit you on the butt on the way out, because you are fired.' The famous fragment is usually quoted as though it were the whole thought.

- THE LEMONS SPEECH. The correct text is: 'When life gives you lemons? Don't make lemonade. Make life take the lemons back! Get mad! \'I don't want your damn lemons! What am I supposed to do with these?\' Demand to see life's manager! Make life rue the day it thought it could give Cave Johnson lemons! Do you know who I am? I'm the man who's going to burn your house down! With the lemons! I'm going to get my engineers to invent a combustible lemon that burns your house down!' Common errors: 'Make life take the lemons BACK!' is right but people drop 'Get mad!'; it is 'a combustible lemon' (singular), 'get my engineers to invent' (not 'have my engineers make'), and 'burn your house down' (not 'burn down your house'). Also: Cave says the SINCERE version earlier, in the 1980s intro — 'When life gives you lemons, make lemonade.' — which is what makes the later inversion land.

- 'You're here because we want the best, and you are it.' is the 1950s line, and it ends 'So: Who is ready to make some science?' The 1970s version is a deliberate callback that collapses: 'So. Welcome to Aperture. You're here because we want the best, and you're it. Nope. Couldn't keep a straight face.' Note the contraction differs between the two ('you are it' vs 'you're it'). Do not blend them.

- 'I'm Cave Johnson. I own the place.' — two sentences, and it is preceded by 'Now, you already met one another on the limo ride over, so let me introduce myself.' It is often quoted as 'Cave Johnson here, I own the place', which conflates it with his other stock opener, 'Cave Johnson here.'

- 'Speedy-thing goes in, Speedy-thing comes out.' — hyphenated and capitalised that way in the script, and it is the layman's-terms gloss that FOLLOWS 'Momentum, a function of mass and velocity, is conserved between portals.' Usually quoted without its setup.

- 'Material Emancipation Grill', not 'Grille'. Portal 2's captions use 'Grill'; a lot of circulating Portal 1 fan transcripts spell it 'Grille'. Use 'Grill'.

- 'The Aperture Science Bring Your Daughter to Work Day is the perfect time to have her tested.' — the Portal 2 caption capitalises it as a proper product name with no quote marks. Older Portal 1 transcripts render it 'Aperture Science \'Bring your daughter to work\' day'. Prefer the Portal 2 form. The full line opens 'Well done! Remember:'.

- 'Thank you for helping us help you help us all.' — three 'help's, in that order. Frequently truncated to 'helping us help you', which loses the joke entirely (the sentence closes the loop back to the company's benefit).

- 'Cake and Grief Counseling will be available at the conclusion of the test.' — 'Grief Counseling' is capitalised and it is 'at the conclusion of the test', not 'after the test'. The preceding sentence is 'The Enrichment Center is committed to the well-being of all participants.'

- 'an unsatisfactory mark on your official testing record followed by death' — Portal 2's caption puts 'unsatisfactory' in single quotes and uses 'followed by death'; the older Portal 1 transcript reads 'Followed by death.' as a separate sentence. Either is defensible; be consistent, and never paraphrase it as 'and then death'.

- THE FOUNDING DATE IS CONTESTED CANON AND YOUR SITE SITS ON THE FAULT LINE. The 2007 aperturescience.com ARG terminal states: '1953 - Aperture Science begins operations as a manufacturer of shower curtains' and '1957 - 1975 - Mostly shower curtains.' Portal 2 (2011) retconned the company much earlier, with a 1950s Cave already running deep underground test shafts. A site dated 1959 is, by the ARG's timeline, in the middle of the shower-curtain years. Decide deliberately which canon you are in and hold it on every page.

- THE EISENHOWER CONTRACT WORDING. Verbatim: '1956 - Eisenhower administration awards Aperture a contract to provide shower curtains to all branches of the military except the Navy.' It is 'all branches… except the Navy' — the Navy exclusion is the joke and is routinely dropped.

- 'Aperture Fixtures', the January 1944 salt-mine purchase, the 1947 rename, and 'Shower Curtain Salesman of the Year' — all four appear in your repo's docs/HOUSE-STYLE.md as hard canon, and I could NOT corroborate any of them from the caption files or the ARG source available in this session. They may be genuine Portal 2 environmental signage that I simply cannot reach (the wikis are egress-blocked here). Verify before the writers build pages on them.

- Cave's sign-off is 'Cave Johnson. We're done here.' — full stop after his name, not a comma. It appears in the Perpetual Testing Initiative DLC, including the memorable 'Cave Johnson, age fifty-one. We're done here.'

- 'Still Alive' is the Portal 1 credits song; 'Want You Gone' is the Portal 2 credits song. Both are sung by GLaDOS. Attributing 'Still Alive' to Portal 2, or quoting 'Want You Gone' as the source of 'We do what we must', is a frequent slip.

- GLaDOS stands for 'Genetic Lifeform and Disk Operating System' — verbatim from the ARG terminal. Note the terminal's gag that the disk-operating half took a decade to reach 'a state of more or less basic functionality' before anyone started on the Genetic Lifeform part. Capitalisation is GLaDOS, with the lowercase 'a'.

- The ARG terminal password is TIER3, and 'Tier 3' is the shower-curtain-adjacent 'rip in the fabric of space' from Cave's dying three-tier R&D memo — not a security level. Sites that reuse it as a clearance level miss the joke.

- 'Rest assured that an independent panel of ethicists has absolved the Enrichment Center, Aperture Science employees, and all test subjects OF ANY moral responsibility…' — Portal 2's caption reads 'of any moral responsibility'; the older Portal 1 transcript reads 'for all moral responsibility'. Prefer 'of any'. Note that the test subjects are absolved too, which is the detail everyone forgets to include.

## 2. SLOGANS AND WALL COPY

- **[verbatim]** “Aperture Science / We do what we must / Because we can / For the good of all of us / Except the ones who are dead”  
  *Lyric from 'Still Alive', the Portal 1 end-credits song sung by GLaDOS. NOT painted on any wall in either game — see corrections. Line breaks as sung.*

- **[verbatim]** “Thank you for helping us help you help us all.”  
  *GLaDOS, Portal 1, Test Chamber 15, following the Cake and Grief Counseling line. The closest thing Portal 1 has to a corporate tagline.*

- **[verbatim]** “Testing is the future, and the future starts with you.”  
  *Enrichment Center Announcer, Portal 2 pre-hub (Announcer_prehub17), addressed to non-employees who discover the facility amid the ruins of civilization.*

- **[verbatim]** “At the Enrichment Center we promise never to value your safety above your unique ideas and creativity.”  
  *GLaDOS, Portal 1, camera-destruction response. Continues: 'However, do not destroy Vital Testing Apparatus.'*

- **[verbatim]** “The Enrichment Center reminds you that bold, persistent experimentation is the hallmark of good science.”  
  *GLaDOS, Portal 2. Reads as a wall-motto and is the best ready-made 'inspirational' line in the corpus.*

- **[verbatim]** “They say great science is built on the shoulders of giants. Not here. At Aperture, we do all our science from scratch. No hand holding.”  
  *Cave Johnson, Portal 2, 1950s track (fifties_elevator_out_a01). Functions as the company's founding boast.*

- **[verbatim]** “Science isn't about WHY. It's about WHY NOT.”  
  *Cave Johnson, Portal 2, 1950s track (fifties_fifth_test_complete09). Capitalisation as in the caption file. The line continues — see corrections.*

- **[verbatim]** “Who is ready to make some science?”  
  *Cave Johnson, Portal 2, 1950s intro. Closing beat of the welcome address.*

- **[verbatim]** “When life gives you lemons, make lemonade.”  
  *Cave Johnson, Portal 2, 1980s track (eighties_intro10), said sincerely before the famous later inversion.*

- **[verbatim]** “Cave Johnson. We're done here.”  
  *Cave Johnson's sign-off, Portal 2 Perpetual Testing Initiative DLC. Also appears as 'Cave Johnson, age fifty-one. We're done here.'*

- **[uncertain]** “The cake is a lie”  
  *Wall graffiti scrawled repeatedly in the hidden 'rat man' dens in Portal 1. It is graffiti by a hiding employee, NOT an Aperture slogan and never spoken aloud by anyone.*

- **[uncertain]** “Aperture Science Innovators”  
  *Company trading name painted on old Aperture signage in Portal 2's salt-mine levels. I could not verify the exact rendering or date range with the wikis blocked.*

- **[uncertain]** “Enrichment Sphere / Test Shaft 09”  
  *Painted facility signage in Portal 2's old Aperture. Wording and typography unverified here.*

- **[uncertain]** “Aperture Science: We Answer to No One”  
  *Sometimes cited by fans as Aperture signage. I could not find it in any caption file and cannot confirm it exists in either game. Treat as fan invention until verified.*

## 7. STYLE GUIDE — writing new copy in this voice

**1. THE CORE ENGINE IS TONAL MISMATCH, NOT JOKES. Never write a punchline. Take an appalling fact and deliver it in the flattest available service register — a notice, a form, a warranty, a welcome. 'That's not part of the test. That's asbestos.' has no joke in it; the comedy is entirely that nobody in the sentence is alarmed. If a line would still be funny read aloud by a comedian, it is wrong. It must only be funny read aloud by a receptionist.**

**2. PUT THE HORROR LAST AND UNSTRESSED. The signature Aperture sentence buries the fatality at the end as a subordinate afterthought, after something trivial: 'will result in an \'unsatisfactory\' mark on your official testing record followed by death. Good luck!' Never lead with the death. Lead with the paperwork. And always follow the horror with something briskly upbeat — 'Good luck!', 'It's true!', 'you are an excellent test subject'.**

**3. THE THIRD PERSON IS THE INSTITUTION'S VOICE; THE SECOND PERSON IS THE VICTIM'S. 'The Enrichment Center reminds you that…' The company refers to itself by name, never as 'we', when it is doing something to you. Switch to 'we' only for promises and gifts ('we promise', 'we are pleased to present'). This split is the whole trick: the company is a place that happens to you, and a firm that generously assists you, in alternating sentences.**

**4. LEARN THE FIVE VERBS OF THE 'REMINDS YOU' FRAME AND ROTATE THEM. The Enrichment Center reminds / regrets to inform / promises / apologizes for / is required to remind / respects your right to / urges you to disregard / is committed to. 'Is required to remind you' is the strongest of these, because it implies an outside authority forcing an unwelcome honesty. Use it for the worst news.**

**5. EUPHEMISE THE NOUN, NEVER THE SENTENCE. Aperture's euphemisms are single compound nouns dropped into otherwise plain prose: 'victory candescence' (being burned alive), 'the euthanizing process' (incinerating a cube), 'unsatisfactory mark' (death), 'ethnological redundancy associate' (executioner), 'compensation vouchers', 'relaxation vault' (a cell with no doors). Coin one crisp bureaucratic compound per page and then use it completely straight, twice.**

**6. INVENT JOB TITLES ENDING IN 'ASSOCIATE'. This is canon and it is inexhaustible: Test Associate, Intubation Associate, Reintegration Associate, Entitlement Associate, Stalemate Resolution Associate, Party Escort Associate. The formula is [abstract noun or absurd procedure] + Associate. Also good: [Noun] Team ('A Crisis Response Team has already been mobilized to deliberate on a response to your crisis'), and [Noun] Annex ('Equipment Recovery Annex', 'Stalemate Resolution Annex').**

**7. PRODUCT NAMES ARE FULL, CAPITALISED AND NEVER ABBREVIATED. 'Aperture Science Handheld Portal Device', not 'portal gun'. 'Aperture Science Material Emancipation Grill'. 'Aperture Science Heavy Duty Super-Colliding Super Button'. 'Aperture Science Vital Apparatus Vent'. 'Aperture Science Emergency Intelligence Incinerator'. The formula is Aperture Science + [overqualified adjective stack] + [mundane object]. The comedy is the ratio of syllables to function. Bolt on a spec if you can: '1500 megawatt'.**

**8. NUMBERS MUST BE ABSURDLY PRECISE AND NEVER ROUND. 'a median latency of forty-four point six years'. 'three of Aperture Science's 176 restrooms'. 'as few as 1.1 volts'. 'Control Group Kepler-Seven'. '4000 degrees Kelvin'. 'sixty percent water… down to twenty or thirty percent'. Precision is how the company signals it has measured the harm carefully and proceeded anyway. Never write 'many', 'several' or 'approximately'.**

**9. CAVE'S SENTENCE RHYTHM: SHORT, SHORT, SHORT, THEN A LONG ONE THAT GETS AWAY FROM HIM. 'Yes, you. Box. Your stuff. Out the front door. Parking lot. Car. Goodbye.' Then, immediately after, a run-on that piles clause on clause until it arrives somewhere he did not intend. He also self-interrupts to correct the record ('The lab boys just informed me that I should not have mentioned the control group'), and addresses Caroline mid-thought as though the microphone were off. He never apologises and never hedges.**

**10. CAVE'S STOCK CHARACTERS ARE FIXED. 'The lab boys' (the scientists, whom he half-trusts and enjoys defying), 'the bean counters' (accountants, whose advice he takes as a dare — 'Bean counters said I couldn't… Did it anyway'), 'the eggheads' (contemptible cowards), and Caroline (competent, addressed directly, praised in terms that would horrify a modern HR department). Black Mesa is 'the competition' and mentioning them makes him lose his temper mid-sentence. Use these names; do not invent substitutes.**

**11. THE 'HOWEVER' PIVOT AND THE 'GOOD NEWS / BAD NEWS' PIVOT ARE THE TWO WORKHORSE STRUCTURES. Pivot one: state a generous corporate value, then void it with a single clause — 'we promise never to value your safety above your unique ideas and creativity. However, do not destroy Vital Testing Apparatus.' Pivot two: 'I've got some good news and some bad news' where the bad news is mild and the good news is catastrophic — the mantis-men line is the template. Both work by making the reader do the arithmetic themselves.**

**12. CITE AUTHORITY THAT CANNOT HELP YOU. Aperture constantly invokes real regulatory machinery — 'state and federal regulations', 'Federal disclosure policies', 'Federal Superfund regulations', 'State and Local statutory regulations', 'an independent panel of ethicists', 'the Laws of Robotics'. The authority is always genuinely invoked and always structurally useless: it compels a disclosure, not a remedy. For a 1959 site this is your richest seam — the Eisenhower contract, House Naval Appropriations, the Atomic Energy Commission, the Interstate Commerce Commission.**

**13. SAFETY LANGUAGE IS USED TO INTRODUCE HAZARDS, NOT REMOVE THEM. 'For your own safety', 'Rest assured', 'Please note', 'Be advised', 'Please be advised that', 'Please refrain from'. Every one of these openers should be followed by something worse than what preceded it. 'have therefore been disabled for your safety' is the canonical example: the safety device was removed for safety.**

**14. THE COMPANY IS PROUD, PATRIOTIC AND ENTIRELY SINCERE. Nothing on the page may wink. No irony, no arch asides, no modern management idiom ('leverage', 'reach out', 'solutions', 'impact' as a verb, 'passionate about'). Cave is a 1950s industrialist who believes he is the hero of the century and is being generous by letting you die for him. The instant a sentence sounds knowing, the whole conceit collapses.**

**15. END SECTIONS ON A COMMAND OR A GOODBYE. Aperture's recordings terminate abruptly and cheerfully: 'Good luck!', 'Goodbye.', 'Now let's solve this thing!', 'We're done here.', 'Let's all stay positive and do some science.', 'You can head on back to your desk.' Never trail off, never summarise, never offer further reading in a warm tone. The recording simply stops, because the next test is waiting.**

**16. FOR A 1959 SITE SPECIFICALLY: exploit dramatic irony the reader owns and the company does not. Cave can boast about moon rocks, brain-mapping, shower curtains for the military, and Caroline running the place, all in perfect innocence. Never let him hint that any of it goes wrong. The reader supplies the dread; the page supplies only the brochure.**
